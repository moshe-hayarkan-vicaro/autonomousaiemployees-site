The AI employee cost model

Tokens per task, times tasks per month, times price per token. The arithmetic is easy. Being honest about the first two numbers is the hard part.

Every role page on this site carries a monthly spend estimate. This page explains where those numbers come from, so you can check them against your own volume instead of trusting a figure someone put in a table.

The short version: an AI employee costs roughly tokens per task × tasks per month × price per token, and the only hard part is being honest about the first two.

The three inputs

Tasks per month. One task is one unit of work: one ticket triaged, one invoice read, one brief written, one pull request reviewed. This is the number people get wrong, usually by overestimating. Count a real week and multiply by 4.3.

Input tokens per task. Everything the model reads: the instructions, the retrieved documentation, the ticket history, the attached document. This is usually much larger than people expect, because retrieval adds context. A support ticket with help-centre articles attached is easily 3,000 to 5,000 input tokens even though the customer wrote 80 words.

Output tokens per task. What it writes back. Much smaller, typically 10 to 20 percent of input. A drafted reply is 300 to 500 tokens. A code review comment is 100. A report is 1,000 to 2,000.

Model tiers, and why the cheapest is usually wrong

Prices move, so the catalog uses three representative tiers rather than naming vendors. The shape of the market has been stable for a while: a small fast tier, a mid tier that most production work runs on, and a frontier tier for the tasks where a wrong answer is expensive.

TierPer 1M in / outWhat it is for
Small / fast$0.15 / $0.60Classification, triage, short drafts. Cheapest per task, weakest on long context.
Mid$1.00 / $3.00The default for most of these roles. Good reasoning at a workable price.
Frontier$3.00 / $15.00Long-context reading, code review, anything where a wrong answer is expensive.

The instinct is to plan against the small tier and be pleasantly surprised. Do the opposite. The small tier is where the quality complaints come from, and the cost difference between small and mid is usually a few tens of dollars a month against a salary. Plan against mid, and treat any saving from routing easy tasks down as upside.

Two things stand out. First, the range is wide: a support agent at 3,000 tickets a month costs an order of magnitude more than an AP clerk at 1,500 invoices, because the ticket volume is higher and the context per ticket is comparable. Second, all of these numbers are small relative to the labour they replace, which is why the interesting question is never “can we afford the tokens” but “is the output good enough to use”.

The four things that move the number

Prompt caching. If a large part of the input is the same every time (system instructions, a style guide, a policy document), caching it typically cuts the input cost by 50 to 90 percent on the cached portion. This is the single biggest lever and it is usually free to enable.

Retrieval discipline. Sending the whole help centre with every ticket is expensive and makes answers worse. Sending the three most relevant articles is cheaper and better. Retrieval quality is a cost decision as well as a quality decision.

Routing. Most tasks in a role are easy. Sending the easy 70 percent to the small tier and the hard 30 percent to mid typically cuts the bill by 40 percent with no visible quality change, provided you can classify reliably.

Batching. Non-urgent work (nightly reconciliation, weekly reporting, bulk classification) can run in batches at a discount. Anything a customer is waiting for cannot.

What is not in these numbers

The estimates cover model inference only. A real deployment also has:

  • Orchestration and tool calls. Every retrieval, API call and database query is either free or billed separately depending on the platform. At high volume this can exceed the model cost.
  • Human review time. The reviewer is the largest real cost in most deployments, and it is the one nobody puts in the spreadsheet. Budget 10 to 20 percent of a person for the first quarter.
  • Setup. Writing the boundary, the documentation, the metric definitions and the approval flow. This is the work that decides whether the deployment succeeds, and it is measured in days, not dollars.

How to sanity-check a vendor quote

Ask for tasks per month, tokens per task, and the model tier. If a vendor quotes a flat per-seat price with no volume assumption, they are either absorbing a lot of variance or they have not modelled it. Both are worth knowing before you sign.

Then multiply it out yourself. The arithmetic is not hard, and doing it once will tell you more than any demo.

Every role page on this site shows its own assumptions in the “estimated model spend” section, so you can substitute your own volume. Start from the catalog.
