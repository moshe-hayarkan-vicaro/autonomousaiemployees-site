Customer Support

IT Helpdesk Agent

An AI employee for internal IT: password resets, access requests, device questions and the same twenty tickets every week.

Internal IT is a better first deployment than customer support for most companies, because the blast radius is smaller and the answers are more stable. Nobody churns over a slow password reset, and the correct answer to 'how do I connect to the VPN' does not change weekly.

The constraint that matters is permissions. An AI employee that can grant access is an attack surface, so the role should be able to request, prepare and verify, but a human or an approval workflow should be the thing that actually grants. Everything else, the triage, the how-to answers, the onboarding checklists, the asset tracking, is safe to automate and is where the hours go.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Access and permission requests

    Takes the request, checks it against the access policy, prepares the grant, and routes it for approval with the justification attached. The approver clicks once instead of reconstructing the request from a thread.

  • Onboarding IT setup

    Runs the new-starter checklist: accounts created, groups assigned, device ordered, software licensed, first-day instructions sent. Reports what is done and what is blocked, per person, before day one.

  • Recurring how-do-I questions

    Answers the twenty questions that make up most of the queue, from your own internal docs, and flags the ones where the docs are wrong or missing.

  • Offboarding verification

    Confirms every account, token and device is actually revoked on the last day, and reports exceptions. The task everyone intends to do and nobody has time to check.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Triages internal IT requests and resolves the ones covered by documented procedure.
  • Prepares access grants and routes them for human approval with justification.
  • Runs onboarding and offboarding checklists and reports exceptions.
  • Answers recurring how-to questions from internal documentation.
  • Maintains an asset and licence inventory from the requests it processes.
  • Flags documentation gaps and policy conflicts it encounters.

What it needs

  • Read access to your identity provider and device inventory.
  • Read access to internal documentation.
  • An approval workflow for anything that grants access.
  • A written access policy, or it cannot check requests against anything.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Granting access. The role prepares, a person or workflow approves.
  • Anything touching privileged or production accounts.
  • Security incidents, which need a human on call.
  • Buying hardware or software.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You have more than about 30 people, so the queue is real.
  • Your access policy is written down.
  • Onboarding is currently a scramble every time.

Not a fit if

  • You have no written access policy. Write it first, the role enforces what exists.
  • You want it to grant admin access autonomously. Do not.
  • You are small enough that IT is one person and a shared doc.

Questions people ask

Is it safe to let an AI employee touch identity systems?

Read access, yes. Write access only through an approval step, and never to privileged accounts. The role's value is that it assembles and checks the request, not that it clicks the button.

Does it replace an IT person?

It removes the queue, which is most of the interruption load. The IT person still owns the systems, the policy and the incidents. Most teams find it makes one person sufficient for longer.

What about the tickets it gets wrong?

It should say it does not know and escalate. A helpdesk agent that guesses is worse than a search box, because people trust it.
