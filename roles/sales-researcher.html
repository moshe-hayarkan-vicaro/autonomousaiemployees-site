Sales Researcher

An AI employee that reads the account before the call: what they do, what changed, who else is involved, and what to open with.

The most expensive hour in a sales team is the one before the call, when a rep is supposed to research the account and instead skims the website for two minutes. Not because reps are lazy, but because real research takes 45 minutes and the calendar does not have 45 minutes in it.

An AI sales researcher does that hour properly and hands back a page. What it cannot do is tell you whether the account is worth pursuing, because that is a judgement about fit and timing that depends on things not written down anywhere. So the role is scoped as preparation: it makes the rep's first five minutes on the call informed instead of generic, and it makes the list they work from specific instead of a filter on a database.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Pre-call account briefs

    One page per meeting: what the company does, how it makes money, what changed in the last quarter, who the likely stakeholders are, and two specific opening angles with the evidence for each. Delivered before the call, not after.

  • Trigger-based list building

    Builds lists from events rather than firmographics: a funding round, a new office, a leadership change, a product launch, a job posting that implies a project. Each row carries the trigger and the date, so the rep knows why the account is on the list.

  • Stakeholder mapping

    Maps who is likely involved in a purchase and what each of them cares about, from public signals: titles, past roles, published priorities. Flags when the map is a guess rather than evidence.

  • Post-call follow-up drafting

    Turns call notes into a follow-up that restates what was agreed, names the next step with a date, and lists the open questions. The rep edits and sends, which takes two minutes instead of twenty.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Produces a one-page brief per meeting with company context, recent changes and opening angles.
  • Builds and refreshes target lists from trigger events rather than static filters.
  • Maps likely stakeholders and their priorities from public information.
  • Drafts follow-ups from call notes with explicit next steps and dates.
  • Keeps a running log of what was learned about each account so nobody re-researches it.
  • Flags when it could not find evidence for a claim instead of filling the gap.

What it needs

  • Read access to the CRM so it knows which accounts are live.
  • Read access to the calendar so briefs arrive before meetings, not after.
  • A web research capability, and a rule about which sources count as evidence.
  • A place to deliver: CRM record, doc, or a channel per rep.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Deciding which accounts to pursue. Fit and timing are judgement calls.
  • The conversation itself, which is the job.
  • Verifying anything that will be said out loud to a customer as fact.
  • Pricing, terms and anything that commits the company.

How to measure itIf you cannot measure it, you cannot tell whether it is working.

MetricHow to read it
Briefs delivered before meetingsShare of meetings with a brief ready beforehand. The adoption number, and the one that predicts everything else.
Rep research hours savedSelf-reported before and after, cross-checked against meeting volume. Rough but directionally reliable.
Meeting-to-opportunity rateCompare accounts with briefs against a control set. This is the number that shows whether better preparation converts.
Follow-up send timeMedian hours from call end to follow-up sent. Under 24 hours is the difference between a live deal and a cold one.

Fit

Good fit if

  • Reps have more meetings than research time.
  • Your CRM is reasonably clean, so it knows what is live.
  • You sell to companies where public information actually exists.

Not a fit if

  • You sell to individuals or very small businesses with no public footprint.
  • You want it to send outbound on its own. That is a different role with a different risk profile.
  • Your CRM is a graveyard nobody updates. It will brief on dead accounts.

Questions people ask

Is this just a data enrichment tool?

No. Enrichment fills fields. This reads the fields, works out what matters for this specific meeting, and writes an argument for how to open. The output is prose with evidence, not a table.

How accurate is the research?

Good on public facts, unreliable on inference. The role is designed to mark its own guesses, and you should treat anything it states without a source as a hypothesis to check in the first minute of the call.

Can it write the outbound emails too?

It can draft them, and the drafts are better than generic templates because they are grounded in the research. Whether it should send them is a separate decision with a separate risk profile, covered under the SDR role.
