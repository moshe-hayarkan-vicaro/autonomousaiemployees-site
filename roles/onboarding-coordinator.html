Onboarding Coordinator

An AI employee that makes sure every new starter has accounts, equipment, a schedule and a person to talk to before their first morning.

Onboarding fails in a predictable way. Not because anyone is careless, but because it is a cross-functional checklist with no single owner and a hard deadline. IT has the accounts, facilities has the laptop, the manager has the schedule, HR has the paperwork, and nobody is watching the whole board. The result is a first day spent waiting for a login.

An AI employee is genuinely good at this because it is pure coordination: track the checklist, chase the blockers, escalate before the deadline rather than after. It does not need to make judgement calls. It needs to know what done looks like for each item and who owns it, and then be relentless about it in a way no busy human is.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Pre-start checklist execution

    Tracks every item from offer signature to first morning: contract, accounts, groups, equipment, payroll, buddy assignment. Chases the owner of each open item and escalates to the manager when something will miss the start date.

  • First-week scheduling

    Builds the first-week calendar: intro meetings, tool walkthroughs, the first real task. Sends the schedule to the new starter and the relevant colleagues, and reschedules when something moves.

  • 30-60-90 day check-ins

    Sends structured check-in prompts at the right intervals, collects the answers, and summarises them for the manager. Surfaces the concerns people do not raise unprompted.

  • Onboarding content maintenance

    Keeps the welcome doc, the tool list and the org chart current, and flags when a documented process no longer matches what people actually do.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Tracks the full pre-start checklist per new hire and chases open items.
  • Escalates blockers to the manager before the start date, not after.
  • Builds and maintains the first-week schedule.
  • Runs 30-60-90 day check-ins and summarises responses.
  • Keeps onboarding documentation current.
  • Reports readiness per new starter and time-to-productivity trends.

What it needs

  • Read access to the HR system and the calendar.
  • A written onboarding checklist with a named owner per item.
  • Access to raise tickets in IT and facilities systems.
  • A manager who responds to escalations.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • The welcome and the relationship. A person should still say hello on day one.
  • Judging whether someone is settling in.
  • Any performance conversation.
  • Decisions about role scope and expectations.

How to measure itIf you cannot measure it, you cannot tell whether it is working.

MetricHow to read it
Day-one readinessShare of new starters with every checklist item complete before their first morning. The headline number.
Time to first real taskDays from start to the first piece of actual work. Measures whether onboarding is real or ceremonial.
Check-in response rateShare of 30-60-90 prompts answered. Low response means the prompts are wrong or the culture is.
Blockers escalated before deadlineShare of at-risk items caught early rather than discovered on day one.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You hire regularly enough that onboarding is a repeated process.
  • Onboarding currently involves a lot of chasing.
  • IT and facilities will accept tickets from an automated system.

Not a fit if

  • You hire once a year.
  • Nobody owns onboarding, so there is no checklist to run.
  • You want it to handle the human welcome. That should stay human.

Questions people ask

Is this just a checklist tool?

The checklist is the mechanism, the chasing is the value. Most companies have a checklist; the failure is that nobody follows up on the open items until it is too late.

What about remote onboarding?

It works better remotely, because the informal corridor onboarding that covers for a broken process does not exist. The checklist becomes the whole experience, which makes automating it more valuable, not less.

Does it replace HR?

No. It removes the coordination, which is the part HR does not have time for. HR keeps the judgement, the policy and the conversations.
