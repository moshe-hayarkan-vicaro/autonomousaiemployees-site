Content Writer

An AI employee that drafts from a brief, in your voice, with your sources, and hands back something an editor can improve rather than rewrite.

The honest version: an AI writer produces a competent first draft and a bad final one. The draft is useful because the hardest part of writing is the first 300 words, and a draft removes that. The final version is bad because the last 20 percent of any good piece is judgement about what to cut, and that is the part no model does well.

So the role is scoped as a drafting role. It works from a brief you approved, in a voice you defined, citing sources you can check. It flags its own uncertain claims instead of smoothing over them. What comes back is a document an editor improves in an hour rather than a blank page they avoid for a week. Teams that treat it as a publisher rather than a drafter get exactly the generic content they deserve.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • First drafts from approved briefs

    Takes a brief with a defined question, angle and section list, and produces a draft that answers it. Cites sources inline and marks any claim it could not verify, so the editor knows where to look first.

  • Repurposing long-form into short

    Turns a published article into a newsletter section, a LinkedIn post and five social snippets, each rewritten for the format rather than truncated. Keeps the argument intact instead of quoting the intro.

  • Refreshing decaying pages

    Takes a page that lost traffic, compares it against what currently ranks, and proposes specific edits: sections to add, claims to update, examples that are now stale. Proposes edits, does not silently rewrite.

  • Voice-matched variants

    Produces three openings for the same piece in different registers so an editor can pick. Cheaper than writing three, and it surfaces the choice instead of hiding it.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Drafts articles, landing copy, newsletters and product descriptions from a brief.
  • Maintains a voice guide from your published work and applies it consistently.
  • Cites sources and flags unverified claims rather than asserting them.
  • Produces format variants of the same argument for different channels.
  • Keeps a style checklist and self-reviews against it before handing over.
  • Logs what it changed on a refresh so the diff is reviewable.

What it needs

  • A brief template and an approval step, or it will invent its own angle.
  • A voice guide, ideally built from five to ten pieces you consider good.
  • Read access to your CMS or docs so it can see what already exists.
  • A source of truth for product facts. Without one it will guess, confidently.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Approving the brief. A bad brief produces a bad draft no editor can save.
  • Fact-checking anything that carries legal, medical or financial weight.
  • The final edit, which is where the piece becomes good.
  • Deciding what the company is willing to say in public.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You have more briefs than writing capacity.
  • You have published work that defines a voice worth matching.
  • An editor is available to review within a day or two.

Not a fit if

  • You want content published without human review. That is how sites get deindexed.
  • Your product facts are not written down anywhere. It will invent them.
  • You need original reporting, interviews or proprietary data. It cannot go and get those.

Questions people ask

Will Google penalise AI-written content?

Google's guidance targets content made to rank rather than to help, regardless of how it was produced. A reviewed, sourced, genuinely useful article is fine. A thousand unreviewed articles are not, and the risk is the same whether a person or a model typed them.

Can it match our brand voice?

It can match a voice that is written down and demonstrated. Give it five to ten published pieces you consider good plus a short list of things you never say, and it gets close. Give it nothing and you get the default register everyone recognises.

Does it replace a writer?

It replaces the blank page. Writers who use it ship more. Teams that use it to avoid hiring writers usually end up with content nobody reads, which is a strategy problem rather than a tool problem.
