Reconciliation Analyst

An AI employee that matches the ledger against the bank, the processor and the subledger, and explains every break instead of listing it.

Reconciliation is a matching problem with an explanation problem attached. The matching is mechanical and a script can do it. The explanation is where the time goes: a 400-dollar break between the ledger and the processor takes twenty minutes of digging to attribute to a refund that was booked in the wrong period.

An AI employee is good at both halves, with one rule. It must never adjust the ledger. It proposes the journal entry, cites the evidence, and a human posts it. That keeps the control intact while removing the part of month-end that everyone dreads, which is the digging.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Bank and processor reconciliation

    Matches transactions across the ledger, the bank feed and the payment processor, and reports unmatched items with the amount, the date and the most likely cause.

  • Break explanation

    For each unmatched item, checks the usual causes in order: timing, fees, refunds, currency, duplicate posting, misclassified account. Reports which one it found and the evidence for it.

  • Month-end close support

    Runs the close checklist, reports which reconciliations are complete, which have open breaks and what is blocking each one, so the close meeting is about decisions rather than status.

  • Proposed journal entries

    Drafts the correcting entry for each break with a description and a reference, ready for a human to review and post. Never posts it itself.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Reconciles ledger, bank and processor data on a schedule.
  • Explains each break with a likely cause and supporting evidence.
  • Drafts correcting journal entries for human review.
  • Tracks recurring break types and reports which upstream process is causing them.
  • Maintains the close checklist and reports blockers.
  • Keeps a full audit trail of matches, breaks and proposals.

What it needs

  • Read access to the ledger, bank feeds and processor reports.
  • A chart of accounts it can reason about.
  • An approval step for journal entries. It proposes, a human posts.
  • A definition of materiality, so it knows which breaks deserve attention.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Posting journal entries. Always.
  • Judging breaks above the materiality threshold.
  • Signing off the close.
  • Anything involving revenue recognition policy.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You have meaningful transaction volume across multiple systems.
  • Month-end close is a scramble every month.
  • Your data is accessible via API or export, not screenshots.

Not a fit if

  • You have one bank account and 50 transactions a month.
  • Your data lives in systems with no export path.
  • You want it to post entries autonomously. That is not a control you want to give up.

Questions people ask

Can it handle multi-currency?

Yes, and it is one of the better use cases because currency breaks are mechanical and easy to explain. It should state the rate it used and where the rate came from.

What if it explains a break wrongly?

It will sometimes. That is why it proposes rather than posts, and why it must cite evidence. A wrong explanation with evidence is easy to correct; a wrong explanation without evidence is a trap.

Does it replace a controller?

No. It removes the matching and digging, which is the junior half of the work. The controller still owns judgement, policy and the sign-off.
