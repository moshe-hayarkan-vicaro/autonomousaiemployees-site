Social Media Manager

An AI employee that keeps the calendar full, adapts one idea to each platform, and drafts replies, while a person owns the voice and the arguments.

Social media is the marketing role most likely to be handed to an AI employee too early, because the output is short and looks easy. It is not easy. The value in a social account is a point of view, and a point of view is the one thing a model cannot supply. What it can do is everything around the point of view: keep the calendar from going empty, adapt one idea into five formats, draft replies so a human can approve in seconds, and tell you which posts actually worked.

The scoping that works is to treat it as a production and measurement role. You supply the opinions, the news reactions and the things worth saying. It supplies the throughput. Teams that invert this get a feed of inoffensive posts that nobody shares, which is worse than posting nothing because it costs money and signals nothing.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Cross-platform adaptation

    Takes one approved idea and rewrites it for each platform's actual conventions: length, hook, formatting, whether links belong in the post or the reply. Not the same text pasted five times.

  • Reply drafting

    Watches mentions and comments, drafts a reply for each, and queues them for approval. Turns a 40-minute daily scroll into a five-minute review, and stops the account from looking abandoned.

  • Weekly engagement reporting

    Reports which posts got reach and which got nothing, with a hypothesis for why. Separates reach from engagement from clicks, because conflating them is how teams convince themselves a post worked.

  • Calendar backfill

    When the calendar has gaps, proposes posts from material you already have: published articles, product changes, support questions worth answering publicly. Fills gaps with substance rather than filler.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Maintains a posting calendar across platforms and flags gaps before they become empty days.
  • Adapts approved ideas to each platform's format and length conventions.
  • Drafts replies to mentions and comments for human approval.
  • Tracks engagement by post type and reports what is working.
  • Mines existing content for post material so the calendar is not invented from nothing.
  • Keeps a list of recurring questions from comments that belong in the FAQ or the docs.

What it needs

  • Publishing access, ideally with an approval queue rather than direct posting.
  • Read access to your content library so it can mine material.
  • Platform analytics, or it will report vibes instead of numbers.
  • A written voice guide and an explicit list of topics you will not touch.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Having the opinions. This is the whole job and it does not delegate.
  • Replying to anything sensitive: complaints, criticism, press, competitors.
  • Approving posts before they go out, at least for the first few months.
  • Deciding which platforms are worth being on at all.

How to measure itIf you cannot measure it, you cannot tell whether it is working.

MetricHow to read it
Calendar fill rateScheduled posts versus planned slots. The most basic measure that the role is doing its job.
Reply latencyMedian time from mention to approved reply. Under a few hours changes how the account is perceived.
Engagement rate by post typeEngagements divided by reach, grouped by format. Tells you which formats to double down on.
Approval rejection rateShare of drafts a human rejects. High rejection means the voice guide is wrong, not that the model is bad.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You have things to say and no time to say them consistently.
  • You already publish content that can be mined for posts.
  • Someone can review a queue once a day.

Not a fit if

  • You want it to generate the company's point of view. It has none.
  • You need real-time crisis response. That is a human on call, not a schedule.
  • Your account is small enough that a person posting twice a week is cheaper.

Questions people ask

Can it post without approval?

Technically yes, and you should not let it for the first few months. The failure mode is not offensive content, it is tone-deaf content on a bad day, which is harder to notice and harder to undo.

Will it sound like a bot?

It will if you give it no voice guide and no examples. With a guide built from your own posts and an explicit list of banned phrases, most readers cannot tell, and the ones who can usually do not care if the content is useful.

Which platforms work best?

The ones where your audience already is and where the format is text-first. Adaptation quality drops on platforms where success depends on visual timing or trend participation, because those need a human watching the feed.
