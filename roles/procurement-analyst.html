Finance & Operations

Procurement Analyst

An AI employee that reads the contracts, tracks the renewals, and tells you what you are paying for that nobody is using.

Most companies have no idea what software they pay for. Not roughly, actually. The contracts are in a shared drive, the invoices are in AP, the seats are in an admin console nobody logs into, and the renewal dates are in someone's head. The result is auto-renewals on tools nobody uses and two products doing the same job.

This is a research and tracking role, which is exactly what an AI employee is good at. It reads the contracts, builds the calendar, reconciles seats against actual usage, and produces a brief before each renewal. It does not negotiate and it does not sign. It makes sure that when someone does negotiate, they know what they are paying for and what the alternatives cost.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Renewal calendar and alerts

    Extracts term, notice period, auto-renewal clause and price escalation from every contract, and alerts before the notice window closes rather than after.

  • Licence and seat waste

    Compares seats paid for against seats actually active in the last 30 days, per tool, and quantifies the annual waste. Usually the fastest payback of any analysis in a company.

  • Duplicate tool detection

    Groups tools by what they actually do and flags overlaps, with the cost of each and which team owns it. The conversation that follows is uncomfortable and worth having.

  • Negotiation brief

    Before a renewal, assembles usage, price history, alternatives and the leverage points: what you would actually switch to, and what that would cost.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Extracts contract terms, notice periods and escalation clauses into a single calendar.
  • Reconciles paid seats against active usage per tool.
  • Identifies overlapping tools and quantifies the duplication.
  • Prepares negotiation briefs with usage data and alternatives.
  • Tracks price changes across renewals and flags unexplained increases.
  • Maintains a vendor register with owner, cost, term and renewal date.

What it needs

  • Read access to contracts, ideally in one place.
  • Read access to AP or the invoice system for actual spend.
  • Admin or read access to the tools' usage data, which is often the hard part.
  • A list of who owns each vendor.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Negotiating and signing. The role prepares, a person decides.
  • Deciding to cancel a tool a team depends on.
  • Security and data-processing review of any vendor.
  • Anything involving a vendor relationship that matters strategically.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You have more than about 20 SaaS tools.
  • Contracts are scattered and renewals surprise you.
  • Someone can act on a cancellation recommendation.

Not a fit if

  • You have ten tools and one person who knows all of them.
  • You have no access to usage data, so waste analysis is guesswork.
  • You want it to negotiate. It has no leverage and no relationship.

Questions people ask

How does it get usage data?

From each tool's admin API or export, which is the practical bottleneck. Where access is missing, it reports the gap rather than estimating, and the gap list itself is usually useful.

Can it read scanned contracts?

Yes, with the same caveat as invoices: it should flag low-confidence extractions rather than guess a notice period. A missed notice window is a real cost.

Is this a finance or an IT role?

Both, which is why it usually goes unowned. Put it where the budget sits and give it read access to both sides.
