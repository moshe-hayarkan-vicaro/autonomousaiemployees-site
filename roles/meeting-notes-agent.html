Admin & Executive

Meeting Notes Agent

An AI employee that turns every call into decisions, owners and dates, and puts them where the team will actually see them.

Transcripts are not notes. A transcript is a record of what was said; a note is a record of what was decided and who is doing what by when. Most meeting tools produce the first and call it the second, which is why nobody reads them.

The useful version of this role is narrow and specific: extract the decisions, the owners and the dates, and put them somewhere the team already looks. Then track whether the actions actually happened, and surface the ones that did not. That last part is the difference between a note-taking tool and something that changes how a team operates.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Decision capture

    Extracts what was actually decided, as distinct from what was discussed, and records it with the date and who was in the room. Distinguishing decision from discussion is the whole skill.

  • Action item tracking

    Pulls out commitments with an owner and a date, files them in the task system, and follows up on the ones that go past due. Most action items die because nobody chases them.

  • Cross-meeting summaries

    Produces a weekly view of what was decided across all meetings, so a decision made in one room does not get contradicted in another.

  • Onboarding and handover notes

    Turns a series of meetings into a written record of how a project actually works, which is the documentation nobody writes until someone leaves.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Extracts decisions, owners and dates from meeting transcripts.
  • Files action items into the task system with an owner and a due date.
  • Follows up on overdue actions and reports them.
  • Produces cross-meeting summaries of decisions made.
  • Keeps a searchable decision log per project.
  • Flags when a new decision contradicts an earlier one.

What it needs

  • Access to meeting transcripts or recordings, with the consent of participants.
  • Write access to the task system and a place to publish notes.
  • A definition of what counts as a decision, agreed with the team.
  • A retention and consent policy, because recordings are personal data in many places.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Confirming that a decision was actually made, when the meeting was ambiguous.
  • Sensitive conversations that should not be transcribed at all.
  • Judging whether an action item is realistic.
  • Any performance or personnel discussion.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You have a lot of meetings and no written decisions.
  • Action items currently live in people's heads.
  • You have a task system the team actually uses.

Not a fit if

  • Your meetings are mostly relationship or sensitive conversations.
  • You have no task system, so actions have nowhere to go.
  • You want transcripts. You want decisions, and that is a different output.

Questions people ask

Is recording meetings a privacy problem?

It can be, and the rules differ by jurisdiction. Get consent, tell people it is happening, and exclude the conversations that should not be recorded. The role should be able to be switched off for a meeting without friction.

How accurate is decision extraction?

Good when a decision is stated clearly, unreliable when it is implied. The design rule is that ambiguous cases become a question in the notes rather than a confident record of something that did not happen.

Does it replace a chief of staff?

No. It replaces the note-taking and the chasing. The chief of staff still decides what matters and handles the conversations that need a person.
