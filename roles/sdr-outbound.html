SDR (Outbound)

An AI employee that drafts grounded outbound and manages the follow-up cadence, with a human approving every send.

Outbound is the role where AI employees have done the most damage to their own reputation. The pattern is well known: a tool scrapes a list, generates a thousand emails with a first-name merge field and a fake compliment, sends them, and burns the domain. That is not an AI employee problem, it is a scoping problem, and it is worth being blunt about because the alternative works.

The version that works is narrower. The AI employee researches the account, drafts a specific message grounded in something real, and queues it. A human approves. The cadence, the follow-ups, the reply triage and the CRM hygiene are all automated, because those are the parts that get skipped when a person is busy. Volume stays modest and reply quality goes up, which is the opposite of what the spray-and-pray tools optimise for.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Grounded first-touch drafts

    Writes each message from a specific, verifiable observation about the account, and states the observation so the rep can check it in two seconds. No fake compliments, no 'I noticed you recently...' when nothing was noticed.

  • Cadence management

    Runs the follow-up sequence: who is due, who replied, who should be dropped. Most outbound fails on follow-up discipline rather than on the first message, and this is the part that is genuinely automatable.

  • Reply triage

    Sorts replies into interested, not now, not a fit, wrong person and out of office, drafts a response for each category, and routes the interested ones to a human immediately rather than in the next batch.

  • Domain reputation guardrail

    Watches bounce and complaint rates and pauses the cadence when they cross a threshold. The single most valuable thing an automated outbound system can do is stop itself.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Drafts personalised first-touch messages grounded in verifiable account research.
  • Runs and maintains the follow-up cadence with clear stop conditions.
  • Triages replies by intent and drafts a response for each category.
  • Keeps CRM records current: stage, last touch, next step, disqualification reason.
  • Monitors bounce and complaint rates and pauses when they degrade.
  • Reports reply rate by message angle, so the team learns which arguments land.

What it needs

  • An approval queue. Every send reviewed, at least until reply rates prove the drafts.
  • A verified, warmed sending domain separate from your primary one.
  • Read and write access to the CRM.
  • A written definition of your ideal customer profile, or it will guess and guess wrong.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Approving sends. This is the control that makes the role safe.
  • Handling interested replies. The handoff should be immediate.
  • Deciding the ICP and the offer.
  • Any claim about competitors, pricing or capabilities.

How to measure itIf you cannot measure it, you cannot tell whether it is working.

MetricHow to read it
Reply rateReplies divided by delivered. The only top-of-funnel number that reflects message quality rather than list size.
Positive reply rateReplies that are not rejections. This is what separates good outbound from noise.
Bounce and complaint rateGuardrails. If these rise, stop and fix the list before touching the copy.
Approval rejection rateShare of drafts a rep rejects. High rejection means the research or the ICP definition is off.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You have a defined ICP and a real offer.
  • A rep can review a queue daily.
  • You have a separate domain to send from.

Not a fit if

  • You want fully autonomous sending at volume. That is how domains get burned.
  • You have no ICP and are hoping the tool finds one.
  • Your product needs a relationship before anyone will take a meeting.

Questions people ask

Will this get my domain blacklisted?

Not if you keep volume modest, send from a separate warmed domain, and let the guardrail pause the cadence when bounces rise. It will if you point it at a scraped list and let it run at a thousand a day. The tool is not the variable, the volume and the list are.

Is AI outbound detectable?

Recipients notice generic outbound, not AI outbound. A message grounded in something specific about their company reads as human because the thinking is human-shaped. A message with a merge field and a compliment about their 'innovative solutions' reads as spam regardless of who wrote it.

Does it replace an SDR?

It replaces the drafting and the cadence discipline, which is most of the hours. It does not replace the judgement about who to target or the conversation when someone replies. Most teams use it to make one SDR cover what used to take two, rather than to remove the SDR.
