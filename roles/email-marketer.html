Email Marketer

An AI employee that segments the list, writes the sequence, tests the subject lines and reports what the send actually earned.

Email is the channel with the clearest feedback loop in marketing, which makes it a good place for an AI employee and a dangerous place for a careless one. Every send is measurable, so the role can learn what works. Every send also lands in someone's inbox, so a mistake is public in a way a bad blog post is not.

The scoping that holds up is to give the AI employee the production and analysis work and keep the list and the offer human. It can segment, draft, test and report. It should not decide who gets mailed, how often, or what you are willing to promise. Those three decisions are where deliverability and reputation live, and they are not reversible once a send goes out.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Lifecycle sequence drafting

    Writes the welcome, activation, re-engagement and win-back sequences, each with a defined trigger, a single job per email and a stop condition. Drafts the whole sequence so a human edits rather than invents.

  • List segmentation

    Proposes segments from behaviour you already track: what people opened, clicked, bought or abandoned. Names each segment by what it means and what to send it, not by an arbitrary date range.

  • Subject line testing

    Generates variants against a stated hypothesis, runs them as a proper A/B with a fixed sample, and reports the result with a confidence note rather than declaring a winner on a 2 percent difference.

  • Deliverability pre-flight

    Checks a draft before send for the things that quietly kill inbox placement: spam-trigger phrasing, image-to-text ratio, link domains, list age, and whether the segment has been mailed too recently.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Drafts campaign and lifecycle emails from a brief with a single defined job per email.
  • Proposes and maintains behavioural segments with a clear purpose for each.
  • Generates and evaluates subject line and preview text variants.
  • Runs pre-send deliverability checks and flags risk before the send, not after.
  • Reports revenue per send, per segment and per sequence, not just open rates.
  • Keeps a suppression and frequency log so nobody gets mailed twice in a week.

What it needs

  • Read access to your ESP's campaign and segment data.
  • Read access to product or revenue data, or the reporting is decorative.
  • A written offer and claim policy, so it does not invent promises.
  • An approval step before any send to a live list.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Who gets mailed and how often. Frequency is a reputation decision.
  • The offer and the claims. Legal exposure lives here.
  • Approving any send to the full list.
  • Handling replies and unsubscribes that come back angry.

How to measure itIf you cannot measure it, you cannot tell whether it is working.

MetricHow to read it
Revenue per thousand sendsThe only number that matters. Open rates are a proxy that has been unreliable since privacy changes.
Unsubscribe and complaint rateWatch this as a guardrail, not a target. A rising complaint rate means the segmentation or frequency is wrong.
Sequence completionShare of entrants who reach the end of a lifecycle sequence. Low completion means the sequence is too long or the job per email is unclear.
Test win rateShare of A/B tests where the variant beat control with a real sample. Below chance means the hypotheses are noise.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You have a list and no consistent sending cadence.
  • You can connect revenue data to sends.
  • Someone approves sends before they go out.

Not a fit if

  • Your list is bought or scraped. No amount of good copy fixes that.
  • You want it to send autonomously to the whole list from day one.
  • You have no product or revenue data to measure against.

Questions people ask

Can it send campaigns on its own?

It can, and for a small, warm, well-segmented list that is sometimes fine. For anything larger, keep an approval step. The cost of a bad send is not the send, it is the deliverability damage that follows for months.

Will it improve open rates?

Probably not much, and open rates are increasingly unreliable as a metric because of privacy-driven prefetching. Judge it on revenue per send and complaint rate instead.

Does it handle unsubscribes and compliance?

It can maintain suppression lists and flag compliance gaps, but the legal responsibility for consent and unsubscribe handling stays with you. Treat its checks as a second pair of eyes, not a compliance programme.
