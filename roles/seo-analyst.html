SEO Analyst

An AI employee for marketing SEO: keyword research, technical audits, content briefs and rank tracking, run on a schedule instead of when someone remembers.

SEO is one of the few marketing disciplines where the work is genuinely repetitive and genuinely measurable. The same five jobs come around every week: find the keywords you are missing, check what broke on the site, brief the next article, look at what moved, and explain why. A person can do all five well. A person usually does two of them, because the other three are boring and the week runs out.

That gap is what an AI SEO analyst fills. Not strategy, not the decision about which market to enter, but the weekly loop that keeps a content programme from quietly stalling. The useful framing is that this role replaces a standing habit, not a headcount. If you already have someone doing this loop consistently, an AI employee makes them faster. If you do not, it is the difference between having a programme and having a folder of unpublished drafts.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Weekly keyword gap analysis

    Pulls your ranking keywords and your competitors', finds the terms they rank for and you do not, and sorts them by whether you could realistically win them. Output is a ranked list with a one-line reason per term, not a 4,000-row export.

  • Technical crawl triage

    Crawls the site on a schedule, diffs against last week, and reports only what changed: new 404s, redirect chains, pages that dropped out of the index, titles that got duplicated. The value is the diff, not the crawl.

  • Content briefs from SERP intent

    Reads the top results for a target term, works out what shape of answer the searcher wants, and writes a brief with the sections, the questions to answer and the internal links to include. A writer can start from it instead of from a blank page.

  • Rank movement explanation

    When a page drops, checks the obvious causes in order, cannibalisation, lost internal links, a competitor publishing something better, a technical regression, and reports which one it found. Says 'I do not know' when none of them fit.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Builds and maintains keyword lists segmented by intent and by how winnable each term is for your domain.
  • Audits on-page basics across the site: titles, meta descriptions, heading structure, internal links, canonical tags.
  • Writes content briefs that specify the question, the angle, the sections and the sources to cite.
  • Tracks rank and traffic movement week over week and flags the pages that changed materially.
  • Drafts the monthly report with the numbers already in it, so a human edits rather than assembles.
  • Keeps a running list of pages that are decaying and need a refresh, ordered by traffic at risk.

What it needs

  • Read access to Google Search Console and your analytics tool.
  • Read access to a rank tracker, or a keyword data source it can query.
  • Read access to the CMS so it can see what is published and what is not.
  • A place to write: a shared doc, a project board, or a Slack channel.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Deciding which markets, products and audiences to target. That is a business call, not a research task.
  • Approving a brief before a writer spends a day on it.
  • Any change that touches live pages. The analyst proposes, a person publishes.
  • Judging whether a ranking page is actually good, which is an editorial opinion.

How to measure itIf you cannot measure it, you cannot tell whether it is working.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You publish content regularly and the research is the bottleneck.
  • You have Search Console and analytics set up, so the feedback loop exists.
  • Someone on the team can approve a brief in under a day.

Not a fit if

  • You have no content programme yet. Fix that first, the analyst has nothing to analyse.
  • You need someone to own SEO strategy and defend it to the board.
  • Your site is a client-rendered app with no server-rendered HTML. Fix rendering before hiring for SEO.

Questions people ask

Can an AI employee do SEO on its own?

It can run the research and audit loop on its own. It should not publish, redirect or restructure a site on its own, because those actions are hard to reverse and easy to get wrong at scale. The working pattern is that it proposes and a person approves.

Does it replace an SEO agency?

No. An agency brings relationships, editorial judgement and the willingness to argue with you about strategy. This role replaces the weekly research and reporting that agencies often charge for and sometimes skip. Many teams run both.

How long before it shows results?

The technical audit findings show up in week one. Keyword and content work shows up in rankings over two to four months, which is the normal lag for SEO regardless of who does it.
