Customer Support

Customer Support Agent

An AI employee that triages every ticket, drafts the answer from your own documentation, and escalates the ones that carry money or anger.

Support is the highest-volume place an AI employee can work, and the easiest place to get wrong. The failure mode is not a wrong answer, it is a confident wrong answer on the small share of tickets that carry real money or real anger. Those are exactly the tickets where a bad answer costs more than every good answer saved.

So the role is scoped around triage and drafting rather than autonomous resolution. It reads every ticket, classifies it, drafts a reply grounded in your own documentation, and routes anything sensitive to a human with the context already assembled. A human approving a draft costs seconds. A wrong refund, a wrong legal statement or a wrong security answer costs a customer. Teams that scope it this way see deflection on the boring majority and better handling time on the rest.

What it handlesFour use cases this role is actually scoped for.

  • Tier-one triage and drafting

    Reads each ticket, classifies intent and urgency, and drafts a reply from your help centre and past resolved tickets. The agent edits and sends, which turns a queue of 200 into a queue of 200 drafts and 20 decisions.

  • Deflection on repeat questions

    Identifies the questions that come back weekly, answers them from documentation, and reports the top recurring questions so you can fix the product or the docs instead of answering forever.

  • Escalation routing

    Routes by what the ticket actually contains rather than by keyword: billing disputes to finance, security questions to the security owner, churn signals to the account manager, with a summary of the history attached.

  • Documentation gap detection

    Flags tickets it could not answer confidently and clusters them, so the help centre gets fixed where it is actually failing rather than where someone guessed it might be.

Scope and access

What it does

  • Classifies every inbound ticket by intent, urgency and sentiment.
  • Drafts replies grounded in your documentation, citing the article it used.
  • Escalates billing, security, legal and churn-risk tickets to the right human with context.
  • Detects and clusters documentation gaps from tickets it could not answer.
  • Keeps tone consistent across channels and languages.
  • Reports deflection rate, handling time and recurring-question trends weekly.

What it needs

  • Read access to your help centre and past resolved tickets. Without them it will improvise.
  • Read access to the helpdesk, and write access only to drafts at first.
  • A written escalation policy: what must never be answered automatically.
  • Product and billing context, or it will answer policy questions from imagination.

What stays humanThe boundary is the design, not a limitation.

Not delegated

  • Anything involving refunds, credits, contract terms or legal statements.
  • Angry customers, especially repeat contacts. Sentiment routing matters more than accuracy here.
  • Security and data-handling questions, which are commitments.
  • Product decisions that come out of support patterns.

How to measure itIf you cannot measure it, you cannot tell whether it is working.

MetricHow to read it
Deflection rateTickets resolved without a human touch, measured on a stable intent mix. Rising deflection with flat CSAT is the goal.
First response timeMedian minutes to first reply. The number customers actually feel.
Draft acceptance rateShare of drafts sent with no or minor edits. Low acceptance means the documentation is wrong, not the model.
Escalation precisionShare of escalations that genuinely needed a human. Over-escalation makes the role pointless; under-escalation makes it dangerous.

Fit

Good fit if

  • You have a help centre with real articles in it.
  • Ticket volume is high enough that triage is a real cost.
  • An agent can review drafts during the day.

Not a fit if

  • Your documentation is thin or out of date. It will confidently repeat the wrong thing.
  • Most of your tickets are bespoke or high-stakes. Triage helps, autonomy does not.
  • You want it to issue refunds. That is a finance control, not a support task.

Questions people ask

Will customers know they are talking to an AI?

Assume yes, and disclose it. The teams that hide it get caught on the one ticket where the answer was wrong, and the trust cost is much higher than the disclosure cost.

Can it handle multiple languages?

Yes, and this is one of the strongest arguments for the role. A small team covering eight languages badly becomes a team covering eight languages consistently, with a human reviewing the sensitive ones.

What about CSAT?

Watch it as a guardrail. Deflection that raises CSAT is working. Deflection that lowers CSAT is a sign the escalation policy is too aggressive, not that the role is wrong.
